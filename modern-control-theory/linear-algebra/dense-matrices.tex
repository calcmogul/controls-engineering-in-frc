\section{Dense matrices}
\index{matrices!dense}

A dense matrix is filled with mostly nonzeroes. Algorithms designed for dense
matrices can use Single Instruction, Multiple Data (SIMD) instructions to
perform parts of a computation in parallel.

\subsection{Solving dense linear systems}

Solving linear systems with a decomposition followed by a triangular solve is
more efficient and numerically stable than explicitly forming the inverse of the
system matrix. Which decomposition to use depends on the system's properties.
In general, use:
\begin{itemize}
  \item LLT for symmetric positive definite systems
  \item LDLT for symmetric positive semidefinite systems
  \item LU for square invertible systems
  \item QR for least-squares solving
\end{itemize}

See
\url{https://libeigen.gitlab.io/eigen/docs-nightly/group__TutorialLinearAlgebra.html}
for more specific guidance.

\textit{Pivoting} is the act of permuting rows and columns during the solve.
Column pivoting and full pivoting (permuting rows \textit{and} columns) improve
numerical stability at the cost of performance.
